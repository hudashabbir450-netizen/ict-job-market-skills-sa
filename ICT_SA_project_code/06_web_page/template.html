Curriculum and Career-Readiness Recommendations

Academia pays more than Industry at Junior level, slightly more at Mid-Level, and about the same at Senior, but Industry pulls ahead at the top. An academic track pays competitively early on, but Industry's C-suite roles (CTO, CIO) out-earn even a Professor or Head of School.
Academia's top skills are almost entirely non-technical. Student Supervision, Research & Publication and Industry Engagement lead, with Teaching and Curriculum Design close behind. Technical skills show up as a specialisation layer, not the core requirement.
Cloud (AWS/Azure/GCP) is demanded at every technical level in Industry, Junior through Senior. It shouldn't sit only in a Cloud Computing elective; a cloud-fundamentals module belongs in core first and second-year units.
The biggest single Industry salary jump is Senior to Manager/Executive. That step needs leadership and stakeholder skills most technical ICT curricula don't teach directly.
ICT Academia relies heavily on casual/sessional employment. Australian university casualisation is around 45% of headcount (NTEU), with up to 80% of undergraduate teaching delivered by casual staff. An academic career often starts with insecure, part-time teaching work, which is a different risk profile to an Industry graduate role.
Manager/Executive roles are a small share of ads but a large share of pay, and carry the most job security. Worth showing students as two distinct, well-paid tracks (deep technical specialist vs people-leader), not one linear ladder.

Methodology, Sources and Limitations

Research Questions

  • RQ1:Which ICT skills and tools show the highest demand across Adelaide/SA job advertisements, and how does this vary by career stage?
  • RQ2:How do required skills, salary bands and employment security differ between ICT Industry roles and ICT Academia roles?
  • RQ3:How does employment basis (permanent/contractor/casual-temp) vary by sector and career level, and what does this suggest for career-readiness advice?
  • RQ4:What do these patterns imply for ICT curriculum design and career-readiness preparation at university level?

Data Sources

Salary & skills benchmarks: SEEK Adelaide IT salary data; CareerOne SA salary reporting; Adzuna labour-market data; Jobs and Skills Australia (Internet Vacancy Index); Paxus 2026 Hiring Market report; Emanate Technology SA market commentary; SA Skills Commission (SASA) shortage reporting.

Academic salary bands: Australian university Academic Level A–E salary scales; TAFE SA enterprise pay data.

Employment basis / casualisation: NTEU analysis of Dept of Education staffing data; The Conversation / Times Higher Education reporting on university casualisation.

All sources accessed August 2026.

Limitations

  1. Synthetic, not scraped: modelled against published aggregate statistics, not primary data collection.
  2. Small cell sizes at some Sector × CareerLevel intersections (e.g. ICT Academia Junior/Beginner, n=10) reduce precision at finer grain.
  3. 12-month posting window (Sep 2025–Aug 2026) can't capture longer-term or seasonal trends.
  4. Company/institution names are fictional archetypes, not real employers.
  5. Headcount-based casualisation statistics aren't directly equivalent to job-ad-volume statistics (see Model Validation).

Model Validation

Overall average salary. Real benchmark (Adelaide IT, SEEK/CareerOne Aug 2026): ~$111,000. The sample card alongside runs ~13% above this, plausibly because the sample's career-level mix is more senior than the true market-wide mix, which includes a large volume of low-paid entry-level/support postings.

ICT Academia casual/temp share. Sample: 41.6% of Academia postings. Real benchmark (NTEU, public universities): ~45% of headcount. Close alignment, though headcount and job-ad-volume are different units.

Ethics Statement

Automated scraping of SEEK, LinkedIn, I Work for SA and Workforce Australia was deliberately avoided, as it breaches those platforms' Terms of Service and typical university research-ethics expectations for data collection. This project instead uses openly published, citable aggregate statistics to calibrate a transparent synthetic sample, a standard, defensible substitute for primary data collection in a student project context.
